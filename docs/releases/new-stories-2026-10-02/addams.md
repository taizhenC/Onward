## Reading copy

### 1. scene

She met her traveling companions in the foyer after a public spectacle. She was twenty-seven. They were pale and disapproving. They had left while she stayed to watch. The sight had made them feel faint and sick.

For her, old stories had covered what was happening in front of her. She had watched animals being killed while thinking of old stories. She could offer little in her own defense. She had not given the bloodshed much thought. Their disapproval was there for her to face.

### 2. dark_moment

That evening, shame came with the reaction to what she had watched. She felt condemned by more than the spectacle. She had a plan for a house among people whose needs went unmet. She could not place the exact beginning of that plan. It had formed slowly while she continued to study and travel. Now she saw that the plan had become a defense for remaining idle.

She had treated further preparation as a reason to postpone doing the work. She had believed that study and travel were preparing her for great things still to come. The useful life she meant to begin stayed in the future. The house existed in the plan. There was still no house where she lived with those neighbors.

### 3. response

She told an old school friend about her plan for the house. Her friend was traveling with the group. She stumbled as she explained what she hoped to do. She was uncertain. She hoped her friend might join her in carrying it out.

She also feared that saying it aloud could leave nothing there to talk about. The plan was to live among neighbors and try useful work beside them. Study had filled her life. She wanted to test some of that learning in daily action. The unfinished plan was now part of a conversation.

### 4. struggle

Her friend brought energy and comfort to the plan. Its details remained unclear. Months later, she visited an existing neighborhood house to seek suggestions. The following winter, the two friends began searching for a place of their own. They explained the plan to people and meant to pay from their own resources. A critic argued that living together could keep them occupied with each other.

They answered that they could live in separate rooms across the neighborhood. He remained unconvinced. That spring, she noticed an old house from a carriage. She searched for it the next day and on further days. She could not find it. She finally gave up the search.

### 5. turning_point

On moving day, she entered the old house with her friend and another woman. They were going to live there. The woman who joined them began the work of keeping the household. Her place in its life soon grew beyond that work. The rooms had become available after difficulties in arranging a lease. The house was the one she had lost and found again.

A later search had brought her back to it, surprised and overjoyed. It had been repaired. Travel photographs and pieces of family furniture were inside. They had taken pleasure in putting their belongings there. The house had a broad hall and an open fireplace. The plan now had rooms they could enter.

### 6. became

On the first night, they forgot to close a side door. They also forgot to lock it. In the morning, they were pleased that nothing had been taken. During the first weeks, her friend began a weekly reading group in their little upstairs dining room. Two of its members came to dinner each week. They helped wash the dishes afterward.

The table was then cleared for stacks of photographs. Their new work included eating, reading, and making room for people at home. Their larger hopes for changing social life remained uncertain in her account. She could not claim that every ambition had been achieved. The guests helped clear the table for those photographs.

### 7. bridge

Her name was Jane Addams.

She co-founded Hull House, where Chicago neighbors could gather, learn, and take part in community life.

Your life is not theirs. But a piece of this story may still sit beside you.

You do not have to know every detail before you can speak. You are allowed to be uncertain.
