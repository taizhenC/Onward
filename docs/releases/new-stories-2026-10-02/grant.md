## Scope and limitations

The active episode begins September/October 1858 at age thirty-six and ends at the first call on April 15, 1861, while he was still thirty-eight. The became passage covers the memoir’s eleven months of ordinary shop work before that call. The primary signed proclamation dates the boundary; it is twelve days before his April 27 birthday. The date bounds the period and does not claim that his last shop shift occurred that day. No military return is narrated. The 1854 resignation and later financial failure are outside this story. The memoir gives May 1860 for the move, whereas some later institutional summaries give April; this draft retains May as the first-person date and does not harden a day. It does not turn salaried work into prosperity, emotional recovery, humiliation, or a last chance. The turning point is a compressed recorded move and clerkship, without a documented arrival-day interaction or sensory scene. That is a real craft limitation, retained rather than fabricated. Sources do not justify a 700-word event-led scene at this turn without repetition, so this draft is intentionally shorter and needs editorial review. Contemporary correspondence explicitly discusses enslaved people on the farm and hiring them out; fact-enslaved-labor is mapped alongside fact-behind to the sentence about overseeing workers, so the reader fold actually retains this morally consequential context. The account must not be marketed as solitary honest toil or made into an uncomplicated poverty hero. No dramatic quotation or diagnosis is invented.

The reading copy has 575 words across seven passages. No direct quotations are used; source speech is paraphrased and mapped as fact. Each sentence carries exactly one evidence treatment. Retrospective memoir material is marked probable. The bridge addresses any reader without predicting an outcome.

## Reading copy

### 1. scene

He came home one evening on a day when the chills were bad. He was thirty-six. He felt very ill. His wife had been worse while he was away. By his return, she had improved to about where she had been before.

His son was recovering after an illness that had affected his hearing. His father-in-law was still unwell. He had left the household and returned to find sickness still part of its life. Coming home did not remove the illness.

### 2. dark_moment

His farm business was getting behind. Illness had kept him from overseeing its workers, much less doing the work himself. He and his wife had both been sick with chills and fever. His son had been dangerously ill. The fever reduced how much work he could do.

There were animals, crops, and tools in the farm operation. He now planned to sell its stock with his father-in-law. Cleared land could be rented out, and a portion of the farm could be sold. That portion included his own place. The things used for work were also things that could be sold.

### 3. response

He wrote to his father about the intended sale. He also wrote about his father’s business offer. He preferred that offer to a place that gave him only a salary. He wanted the chance of one day doing business for himself.

He liked the idea that some of his income could depend on his own work. His ability to manage a business mattered to that hope. He was planning a visit toward the next spring. The offer and the farm sale were both on the page.

### 4. struggle

That autumn, the stock, crops, and farming tools went to auction. He gave up farming. In winter, he entered a partnership selling property. By spring, he still could not tell whether it would succeed. He offered to send cards for his father to pass to possible customers. He enclosed a list of property and prices.

The business had enough work for one person. Its income could not support two families. He applied for a county job and did not receive it. Later that year, he was still unemployed. He expected another appointment, but had not received it when he wrote.

### 5. turning_point

He took a clerkship in his father’s shop. He had left the property partnership and moved to another town. His two younger brothers were already in the business. The work now gave him a fixed salary to support himself and his family.

His father had intended to put all three sons into the business. The brother who had built it up was seriously ill. The larger change in the business was being postponed while he remained in that condition. The place available to him was the clerkship.

### 6. became

He attended closely to the work in the shop. Most of the people he came to know were customers or worked in the same trade. The salary supported his family. It was fixed pay, although he had earlier preferred a chance to work for himself.

His brother’s illness remained part of the family business. The intended change still had to wait. For this part of his life, the ordinary work was in the shop.

### 7. bridge

His name was Ulysses S. Grant.

He became a president of the United States.

Your life is not theirs. But a piece of this story may still sit beside you.

You are allowed to need help from other people. You do not have to call that failure.

## Passage treatment

- **scene:** The located return home from October 1 letter is one evening; his age is calculated from memoir birth. The family’s partial improvement is retained as a fact, not turned into an invented mood.
- **dark_moment:** The current illness and planned farm sale are weighed against explicitly earlier September inability to work. Earlier material is grammatically marked as past context, not moved onto an unrecorded October day.
- **response:** One letter to his father is the small action; plans and preferences remain plans. Sending a letter does not invent acceptance of the offer in 1858.
- **struggle:** Only this passage compresses auction, partnership and job searches. The October 1859 expected appointment is not narrated as received.
- **turning_point:** A documented move and clerkship supply the turn, but the memoir does not stage the arrival. This is a compressed-turn craft exception needing human review. No invented counter, smell, greeting, shame or rescue dialogue.
- **became:** Ordinary paid shop work and postponed business changes across the memoir’s eleven months before the first call. The signed April 15, 1861 proclamation bounds that period before his thirty-ninth birthday. No later military comeback is narrated. The brother’s illness remains a difficulty within the episode.
