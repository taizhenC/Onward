## Reading copy

### scene

He met his editor on the stairs outside the newspaper office. He was about twenty-four. A woman followed the editor down, striking him with her basket. She had washed his clothes and had not been paid. He had worked for this man, too. His second pay day had arrived without his wages.

He had answered an advertisement and begun filling the local column. He had found stories and made notes. The washerwoman was trying to collect what the editor owed. He left with two weeks of work unpaid. He took his experience with him, and a large dog.

### dark_moment

By evening he sat on steps below a clock with its face lit. His dog lay at his feet. The dog had persuaded a cook to give it food. He had watched from a corner. He had no supper waiting. A whole day of trying to sell books had brought no money.

He thought of the years since he had arrived in this country. He had worked and moved and tried again. From these same steps he had left hungry three years earlier. Then he had carried a little money. Now he had none. He felt his life had been wasted. He could find no hope or purpose in it. His fingers drummed on the book in his hands.

### response

He walked across the street with his former teacher. The man had recognized him on the steps. He had asked what he was doing there. He had explained the day of selling. The teacher knew a news agency looking for someone it could train. He offered to write an introduction.

He followed the teacher into the school office and collected the letter. He had already tried newspaper work. That work had left him with no pay. This was another place, with another editor. The letter would put him before that editor. He left the school with it.

### struggle

The teacher offered his dog a home he could not provide. He agreed to bring it the next day. That night he walked with the dog, their last night together. At sunrise he washed his face and hands at a drinking trough. He pulled his clothes into shape. Then he took the dog to its new home.

After that parting he carried the letter to the news office. The editor looked him over with doubt and allowed him to try. He waited at a desk while the editor prepared the assignments. He had no wages from the paper he had left. He had failed to earn enough selling books. Now he waited for the work of this day.

### turning_point

He went to a public lunch for his first assignment. Food was spread before him. He could see it and smell it. He had not eaten, but he asked for nothing. He watched the gathering and wrote his report.

The editor read what he had written. Then he told him he would do. He gave him the desk and told him to report each morning. The trial had become work he was expected to return to. His first newspaper employer had never paid him. This editor had read his report and accepted him. He had a place to come back to.

### became

That evening he went to a family boarding house. He had work and wages now, and could pay. On the stairs his strength gave out. He collapsed and lay there until someone found him. They carried him inside.

The work that followed was hard and poorly paid. His assignments took him across the city. The working day began in the morning and ran into the next early morning. A desk had been given to him. His body had still needed help getting through a door.

### bridge

His name was Jacob August Riis.

He became a reporter and writer documenting poverty and housing conditions.

Your life is not theirs. But a piece of this story may still sit beside you.
