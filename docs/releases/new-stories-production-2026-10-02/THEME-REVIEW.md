Jacobs's `self_invention` is a thematic inference from the supported move into an unfamiliar northern life after prolonged concealment. Her preparations, assisted departure, voyage, mistrust, relief in open air and arrival among strangers are documented in [*Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl*, chapters XXIX–XXXI](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11030/pg11030-images.html). The label must remain an editorial connection to beginning another life. It supplies no new assertion about her private identity, legal freedom or a subjective transformation. Her legally unfree status, coercion, bodily costs, separation from children and dependence on assistance remain explicit in the existing story and record.

Riis's `late_start` is supported by his retrospective assessment that the previous three years had been wasted, followed by the introduction and reporting trial in [*The Making of an American*, chapter V](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/6125/pg6125-images.html). It means perceived lost time and a stalled beginning, not old age. The existing vocabulary already applies the term to young adulthood: the installed Hurston stage covers ages 26–30. The new label does not convert accepted but unpaid work into creative rejection, make the job an earlier childhood calling or remove the help supplied by his teacher and editor.

The proposal correctly leaves the unsupported Riis `creative_dismissal` tag absent and retains Washington's zero-keyword probe as an unresolved lexical coverage gap. Neither tag additions nor publication can repair Keller's actual age-six distance from ordinary adult intake. Those limitations need honest evaluation and release documentation; they do not justify changing ages, routes, gold labels or the recipe.

Reviewing the sources and changes establishes thematic support and bounded scope. The unchanged original benchmark and separate frozen new-story probes still need measurement against the final production library before publication.
