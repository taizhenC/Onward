## Benjamin Franklin

781 words. Episode ages 21-22. [Research and completion](./franklin_b.md). Candidate SHA-256: bed180661652b7f0892baa9ae3b24f1af0027342dacc6901d3335c96d5dd7fc4.

### scene

He put his head out of the printing shop window. He was about twenty-one. A loud noise outside had drawn him away from his work. He wanted to see what was happening. The neighbors had come to their windows for the same reason.

His employer was down in the street. The man looked up and saw him. Then he called out loudly for him to get back to work. His tone was angry, and there were words of reproach too.

The neighbors could hear how he was being treated. His employer came up into the shop.

### dark_moment

The quarrel continued inside the printing shop. The public scolding had made him angry. Having the neighbors hear it had made the words sting more. Now harsh words passed between him and his employer. They came from both sides.

He had harsh words for the other man too. His employer gave him notice that his job was ending. Their agreement required a quarter of a year before he had to leave. The man said he wished he did not have to give that much notice.

This was the printing shop he had worked to bring out of confusion. It was also where he had helped the other workers learn how to do their jobs. His employer now wanted him gone.

### response

He told his employer he did not need to wait. He told his employer he was leaving that instant. He took his hat and walked out of the shop. Below, he saw another worker he knew. Some of his things were still in the printing shop.

He asked the worker to care for the things that were still in the shop. He also asked him to bring those things to the place where he was staying. The man he had asked was still working for the same employer.

His belongings had been left behind.

### struggle

That evening, the worker came to his lodgings and they talked. He was considering going home. His companion proposed that they open a printing business together. He said he had no money to put into it.

The other man said his father could supply the money. He agreed to the partnership and gave the father a list of equipment. The equipment was ordered, but he still needed work while it was coming. He tried the other printing shop.

There was no job for him there. For a few days, he had no work. Then a civil message from his former employer asked him to return. His companion encouraged him to accept, and he went back.

After a printing job away, he returned and their new equipment arrived. They left with their employer’s consent and rented a house.

### turning_point

An acquaintance brought a customer into the new printing shop. The acquaintance had met the customer in the street asking for a printer. They had barely finished putting their equipment in order.

He and his partner had rented this house together. They had taken in another family to share the cost. All their available cash had gone into the things the business needed. Now this customer was there with work for them.

The man made a payment to the partners. It was the first money their new business brought in. The payment came just when they needed it. He felt great pleasure at receiving it.

He also felt grateful to the acquaintance who had brought the customer. The rented house had become a place where someone came for printing. Their first customer had paid.

### became

The new shop brought a difficult conversation at its door. A stranger asked whether he was the young man who had opened it. He said he was. The stranger said the business cost too much and the money would be lost.

He described troubles he believed were happening or still to come. The talk left the young printer feeling low. They shared the rented house with a family to help cover its cost. A friend found them part of a book to print.

The pay was low, and they worked hard. He prepared a sheet each day while his partner printed it. Other small jobs put that work behind. One night, an accident scattered the printing material for two pages.

He put them together again before going to bed. He had a shop, work, and a share of the rent.

### bridge

His name was Benjamin Franklin.
He was a printer who helped draft the statement that announced the United States as a country independent of British rule.

## Joshua Slocum

774 words. Episode ages 47-51. [Research and completion](./slocum_j.md). Candidate SHA-256: a0a5c8a53c18fa97667ab1337f69a559c055c265a0ae5d4d003236c71ac778b1.

### scene

He found the boat propped up in a field, covered with canvas. He was about forty-eight. It stood some distance from the water. He had come to see the vessel an acquaintance had offered him.

The offer also included help getting her ready for sea. Now he could see what had been offered. The boat was old. She had already spent years there under another man's ownership.

Neighbors had been asking what that man meant to do with her. His arrival brought fresh talk about the old hull. Some thought he was breaking her up. He was there to rebuild her. The boat remained on land.

### dark_moment

His first blow pressed cotton into a seam in the wooden hull. He thought he had struck it correctly. Other people watching thought it was wrong. The hull had been put together, and the rooms had been divided.

Now he was sealing the spaces between the planks. Some feared failure at this stage of his work. He gave thought to bringing in a professional for the job.

One passing man warned about cotton moving out of the seam. Another man repeated the warning when he saw the cotton being driven in. A man regarded as an authority asked whether the same thing might happen.

The comments were about the material he was putting into the boat. His own judgment of that first blow was being questioned. The cotton was in the seam.

### response

He drove another thread of packing fiber over the cotton. This was the layer he had intended to put there from the start. Thick pine boards formed the sides he was sealing, held together with bolts.

The first layer was cotton. The second lay above it in the seams. He was doing the work with the hull already joined and divided into rooms. The planks had been fitted closely along their inner edges.

Along the outer edges, he had left space for the sealing material. He drove the fiber into those spaces. The sealing work continued.

### struggle

Earlier, he had found that shipyard work required a payment to a society. He could not obtain work there without paying first. He had also considered finding a ship to command. There were not enough ships to go around.

He had accepted the offer of the old boat and help rebuilding her. The building work began in earnest while the weather was cold. He cut a nearby oak for the long beam under the hull.

A farmer hauled it and other wood to the site. He steamed straight young trees and bent them over a log for the curved frame. Spring flowers came while he worked, followed by cherries.

Putting the thick pine planks on the frame was tedious. He fastened their ends with bolts passing through the wood. The boat took thirteen months of his own labor.

### turning_point

He sailed the rebuilt boat across a bay with the man who had given her to him. Her mast and sails had been fitted for the short trial. The sealing work was complete, and the boat had been painted.

She had entered the water the day after her rudder was fitted and painted. Now she was sailing across the bay. The short trial went well. He had built a cabin with a place to sleep beneath the deck.

There were shelves for small things and a place for the medicine chest. Another space had been left for food and water. He had made the hull as strong as wood and iron could make it.

The cotton had not moved out of the seams as the watchers predicted. He was afloat in the boat he had rebuilt.

### became

After the trial, people along the shore still asked whether the boat would pay. The materials alone had cost him more than five hundred dollars. That figure did not include thirteen months of his own labor.

He also took occasional work fitting other vessels farther down the harbor. Those jobs kept him there beyond the time spent building his own boat. Afterward, he spent a season fishing along the coast in the new craft.

The rebuilt boat did not make him a successful fisherman. He found that he lacked the skill to bait a hook properly. He had finished a boat. Fishing had not worked.

### bridge

His name was Joshua Slocum.
He wrote a book about sailing around the world alone in the wooden boat he had rebuilt during months of work.

## Mary Somerville

766 words. Episode ages 46-50. [Research and completion](./somerville_m.md). Candidate SHA-256: 3e13e429368a9d627d6b612593e2888fd1e20a41926873001310be6242cbb82d.

### scene

A visitor had come to speak with her at home. She was forty-six. He wanted her to explain a difficult book about the motion of the planets. Her husband joined him in asking her to try. She explained what the work required.

Its readers needed to know a kind of advanced mathematics. Before the main subject, she needed to prove problems about movement and the skies.

The original book had no diagrams. Readers already trained in its mathematics did not need them. For the book he wanted, she said, diagrams were necessary. She was describing the work to the two men asking for it.

### dark_moment

In the conversation, she said she feared she could not do the task. The request had surprised her. She thought the visitor had mistaken how much she knew. Her knowledge had come through her own studies.

She believed it fell far short of the knowledge of men trained at universities. To write on this subject seemed too much to attempt. She doubted not only this book, but writing on any subject at all.

She had not had the university education she was comparing herself against.

She could describe the proofs the book needed. She could explain why its readers needed diagrams. That did not remove her stated fear about doing the work herself. The two men were asking her to try.

She was afraid she was incapable of the task.

### response

She agreed to make the attempt. She promised to do her best. But she set conditions for the two men asking her. Her part in the work had to remain secret. If the manuscript failed, she wanted it destroyed.

The two conditions were part of her answer.

The agreement was to try the writing, with the possibility of failure still there. She had already explained the proofs and diagrams it needed. She did not claim she could make the whole subject simple for everyone.

She accepted the task with the condition of secrecy.

### struggle

She rose early and arranged family work to leave time for writing. There were many interruptions. Her small room had no fireplace. In winter she had to write in the room where guests were received.

Visitors could arrive while she was in the middle of a difficult problem. They had come a long way to see her. She felt she had to receive them.

When the bell announced a visitor, she hid her papers to keep the work secret. She learned to leave a subject and resume it. She also taught her children, answering their questions beside her own work.

The writing took a considerable time. When it was finished, she sent the manuscript for examination and criticism. She was very nervous while it was being examined.

### turning_point

The astronomer sent her a letter about the manuscript. He had read it with pleasure and admiration. He called it useful work. There were also points he wanted her to look at again.

One explanation of how forces act was unclear to him. He had marked it. His note concerned an explanation of a mathematical rule. On an early page, he had made another small note.

He thought this smaller point might hardly be worth a mark on the manuscript.

She was surprised that he had found so few errors. The letter made her happy and proud. The manuscript she had offered for examination had received his praise. It had also received his corrections.

The praise and the marked explanation were in the same reply. She preserved the letter.

### became

Further criticism came in a later letter. The astronomer said the opening needed more space and fuller explanations. To him, her knowledge had led her to pass over difficulties for beginners.

He asked her to make the principles clearer. Some principles had been used before their basis was explained.

The book was published. A letter thanked her for copies sent to a college library and a learned society. Young students of mathematics could have access to it there.

The writer thought it could help them once they were able to read it. For general readers, only the opening part of the book was understandable. Reading the rest required a knowledge of advanced mathematics.

The difficult mathematics remained in the full book.

### bridge

Her name was Mary Somerville.
She wrote a book explaining the mathematics behind the movements of the planets to people who wanted to understand the skies.
