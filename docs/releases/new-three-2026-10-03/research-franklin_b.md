# Benjamin Franklin: a dismissal, an unsuccessful job search, and a return to work

Research completed October 3, 2026. This is a source-grounded candidate investigation, not publication approval. The episode is narrowed to approximately 1727–1728, when Franklin was about twenty-one to twenty-two. It ends during the partnership printing shop's difficult beginning; the 1730 ownership change is outside the narrative. The first customer's payment is the turning-point scene.

## Sources opened

- [Franklin, *Autobiography*, Part I, Project Gutenberg 148](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/148/148-h/148-h.htm). This is the public-domain original account in the 1909 Harvard Classics edition. The episode begins in the paragraph starting `But, however serviceable I might be` and continues through the first customer's payment, starting `All our cash was now expended`. Paragraph-opening locators below remain usable when page layout changes. Part I was written in 1771, decades after the episode. Memory-based claims receive probable confidence; the account does not independently prove others' motives.
- [Library of Congress, January 17: Benjamin Franklin](https://www.loc.gov/item/today-in-history/january-17/). The owning institution gives birth January 17, 1706, a shop by 1728, and the Junto in 1727. Franklin's own February 1726–7 passage says he had just passed his twenty-first year, establishing the adult chronological lower bound.
- [National Park Service, Benjamin Franklin's Resume](https://home.nps.gov/inde/learn/historyculture/people-franklin-resume.htm). The park dates the partnership office to 1728. Its Printer and Statesman and Diplomat sections also support the bridge's single public role: a printer who served on the committee drafting the Declaration of Independence. This is institutional chronology, not evidence for feelings inside the shop.
- [National Archives, Hugh Meredith to Benjamin Franklin, July 14, 1730](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Franklin/01-01-02-0052). The original signed dissolution identifies the real partnership and its later end. The modern editorial biography is not used as first-person scene evidence.

## Decisions before writing

The 1730 debt crisis lacks enough attested physical staging to make four separate moments without inventing a court-paper or creditor encounter. It is not used as the dark moment. The earlier window and dismissal episode supplies bodily actions, attested anger, a first response, and an actual failed attempt to find work. The originally considered return-to-work turn stays in compression: the source supplies a sequence rather than a detailed arrival scene. The first customer's arrival and payment supply the stronger single-place turn with an explicitly attested feeling.

The draft will carry no generated quotations and no unrecorded interior life. Before the bridge it will omit names, years, cities, institution names, and distinctive historical equipment. Generic shop, window, hat, belongings, list, and printing work suffice. The memoir's claims about the employer's business motives are not narrated as proved causes. Its retrospective status belongs in the fact confidence and fold notes, not in repeated provenance clauses inside the drama.

Revision 3 includes one later-life fact as biographical identity context in the actual source fold. The used identity atom names Franklin and separately records his memoir's account of a son's death at age four in 1736. The identity source and the Part III paragraph beginning `In 1736 I lost one of my sons` have separate bounded references. The broader atom is `context` and `probable`, retaining the retrospective qualification for the later event. The bridge's name sentence identifies the person; it does not assert that the death happened during the printing episode. The source note makes that distinction explicit. No standalone bereavement fact is attached to a printing sentence, and no prose or runtime projection rule is changed.

Actual current-main composition now projects that identity-context atom, including the later loss and its separate locator. The recipe's later-life fold clause is fulfilled by this displayed biographical context. The content profile again has a death-or-grief flag and states that the fold records a young son's death years after the episode. The later medical argument, cause-of-death discussion, and advice are omitted.

Revision 2 clarifies the acquaintance as the person who found the first customer.
