# Joshua Slocum: rebuilding an old boat, 1892 to before April 1895

Research completed 2026-10-03 for a new Onward candidate. Draft prose and publication are separate stages.

## Primary record opened

[Joshua Slocum, *Sailing Alone Around the World* (1900), Chapter I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/6317/6317-h/6317-h.htm) is Slocum's first-person published recollection. Chapter I begins with a midwinter 1892 encounter and describes the rebuilding, caulking, launch, trial sailing, and thirteen months of his own labor. Chapter II supplies a fishing season that failed before the dated April 24, 1895 departure. The book is a public-domain text, accessed as an actual readable transcription rather than a search summary. The [Canadiana record for the 1900 Century edition](https://www.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.34188) confirms the publication and authorship.

The archive-owning [Martha's Vineyard Museum finding aid, Record Unit 336](https://mvmuseum.org/fa_pdfs/RU%20336%20--Joshua%20Slocum.pdf), Historical Note, p. 2, records February 20, 1844 as the birth date and disappearance after a 1909 departure. Only the birth date and qualified disappearance are used. Its claim that the 1895 voyage departed Vineyard Haven conflicts with Slocum's own Boston departure; that port claim is excluded.

## Claims and bounded locators

- The gift encounter is explicitly midwinter 1892. Slocum accepted an acquaintance's offer of a boat and assistance. He reports an upfront society payment as a barrier to obtaining shipyard work, and a shortage of ships to command. Locator: Chapter I, paragraphs beginning “One midwinter day of 1892” and “They included all the assistance”.
- The next day he found the old boat propped in a field, under canvas and away from salt water. Locator: Chapter I, “The next day I landed at Fairhaven”.
- He felled a nearby oak for the keel; a farmer hauled it and other framing timber. He built a steam apparatus, dressed straight saplings, steamed them, and bent them around a log until set. Locator: Chapter I, “My ax felled a stout oak-tree”.
- The work began in earnest near March in cold weather. Construction continued as trees flowered and fruit appeared. Applying thick pine planks was tedious. Their outer seams were open, while inner edges were close. Locator: Chapter I, “It was hard upon March”, “The seasons came quickly”, and “The planks for the new vessel”.
- At the first caulking blow, observers thought his action wrong. Some feared he would fail; he considered hiring a professional. They predicted that the cotton would move out of the seams. Locator: Chapter I, “The hull of my vessel being now put together” and the following paragraph.
- He drove a thread of oakum above the cotton, an action he says he had intended from the start. The text does not say criticism caused the action. Locator: Chapter I, “However, I drove a thread of oakum”.
- The text records completed caulking, two coats of bottom paint, two of upper paint, fitting and painting the rudder, and launching the following day. The boat floated at its old anchor. Locator: same paragraph beginning “However, I drove”.
- A mast and sails followed; Slocum and the donor made a successful short trial trip. Neighbors still questioned whether it would pay. The materials cost and labor count are recorded, as are intermittent fitting jobs that prolonged his time there. Locator: Chapter I, “Then the mast” and “I was several months more”.
- Slocum then spent a season fishing and judged that he lacked the skill to bait a hook properly. Locator: Chapter II, first paragraph, “I spent a season in my new craft fishing”.
- Slocum authored the memoir of his solo voyage. Title page and Chapter XXI's June 27, 1898 arrival establish the bridge's single plain public role. The voyage itself is excluded from the drama.
- Later disappearance is included in the fold as an unresolved later-life fact. The museum finding aid does not establish the precise date or cause of death.

## Editorial limits and eligibility

The proposed episode has four distinct documented moments: arrival beside the covered boat; the first disputed sealing blow; the planned application of a second fiber; and the short successful sailing trial with the donor. The launch is already-completed context in the trial passage: the account records it passively and does not specify Slocum's launch-day bodily action. There is no documented failed rebuilding operation. The conditional barrier to shipyard work supplies a personal-scale impediment in the compressed struggle, and the failed fishing season supplies an unresolved livelihood cost in became. Criticism alone is not promoted to a technical failure.

The memoir does not attest despair, shame, financial ruin, homelessness, a marital crisis, or a moment of inward courage. None will be added. The low is practical uncertainty about sealing the hull, with the figure's actual consideration of professional help. No invented gesture, quotation, or causal motive is needed.

The birth date makes the initial midwinter scene approximately forty-eight, possibly forty-seven before February 20. The episode is bounded from 1892 to before April 24, 1895, giving a conservative stage age span 47–51. Neither an exact launch year nor an exact date for the failed fishing season is asserted.

All rebuilding details are later recollections and will have probable confidence with that provenance visible in fact statements and passage source notes.
