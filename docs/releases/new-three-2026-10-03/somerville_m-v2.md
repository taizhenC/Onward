## Draft prose

### scene

A visitor had come to speak with her at home. She was forty-six. He wanted her to explain a difficult book about the motion of the planets. Her husband joined him in asking her to try. She explained what the work required.

Its readers needed to know a kind of advanced mathematics. Before the main subject, she needed to prove problems about movement and the skies.

The original book had no diagrams. Readers already trained in its mathematics did not need them. For the book he wanted, she said, diagrams were necessary. She was describing the work to the two men asking for it.

### dark_moment

In the conversation, she said she feared she could not do the task. The request had surprised her. She thought the visitor had mistaken how much she knew. Her knowledge had come through her own studies.

She believed it fell far short of the knowledge of men trained at universities. To write on this subject seemed too much to attempt. She doubted not only this book, but writing on any subject at all.

She had not had the university education she was comparing herself against.

She could describe the proofs the book needed. She could explain why its readers needed diagrams. That did not remove her stated fear about doing the work herself. The two men were asking her to try.

She was afraid she was incapable of the task.

### response

She agreed to make the attempt. She promised to do her best. But she set conditions for the two men asking her. Her part in the work had to remain secret. If the manuscript failed, she wanted it destroyed.

The two conditions were part of her answer.

The agreement was to try the writing, with the possibility of failure still there. She had already explained the proofs and diagrams it needed. She did not claim she could make the whole subject simple for everyone.

She accepted the task with the condition of secrecy.

### struggle

She rose early and arranged family work to leave time for writing. There were many interruptions. Her small room had no fireplace. In winter she had to write in the room where guests were received.

Visitors could arrive while she was in the middle of a difficult problem. They had come a long way to see her. She felt she had to receive them.

When the bell announced a visitor, she hid her papers to keep the work secret. She learned to leave a subject and resume it. She also taught her children, answering their questions beside her own work.

The writing took a considerable time. When it was finished, she sent the manuscript for examination and criticism. She was very nervous while it was being examined.

### turning_point

The astronomer sent her a letter about the manuscript. He had read it with pleasure and admiration. He called it useful work. There were also points he wanted her to look at again.

One explanation of how forces act was unclear to him. He had marked it. His note concerned an explanation of a mathematical rule. On an early page, he had made another small note.

He thought this smaller point might hardly be worth a mark on the manuscript.

She was surprised that he had found so few errors. The letter made her happy and proud. The manuscript she had offered for examination had received his praise. It had also received his corrections.

The praise and the marked explanation were in the same reply. She preserved the letter.

### became

Further criticism came in a later letter. The astronomer said the opening needed more space and fuller explanations. To him, her knowledge had led her to pass over difficulties for beginners.

He asked her to make the principles clearer. Some principles had been used before their basis was explained.

The book was published. A letter thanked her for copies sent to a college library and a learned society. Young students of mathematics could have access to it there.

The writer thought it could help them once they were able to read it. For general readers, only the opening part of the book was understandable. Reading the rest required a knowledge of advanced mathematics.

The difficult mathematics remained in the full book.

### bridge

Her name was Mary Somerville.
She wrote a book explaining the mathematics behind the movements of the planets to people who wanted to understand the skies.

Your life is not theirs. But a piece of this story may still sit beside you.

You do not have to feel certain about your work to care about it. You are allowed to have doubts.
