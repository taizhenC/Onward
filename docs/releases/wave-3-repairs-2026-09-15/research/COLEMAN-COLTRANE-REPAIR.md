## Coleman

### Material actually reopened

- [Coleman's interview in the Chicago Defender, October 8, 1921, page 2,
  transcribed in the Smithsonian conversation kit, printed pages 19–20](https://womenshistory.si.edu/sites/default/files/documents/bessiecoleman_conversations.pdf).
  Both pages were visually inspected in their entirety. Page 19, under
  *Not Satisfied Yet*, reports her first French school's refusal, move to
  another school, course completion, examination and pass, followed by more
  flying and later lessons. Page 20, under *Better to Fly High*, reports the
  witnessed pupil death, shock, continued training, signed risk contract, and
  habitual walk to school. This is her retrospective account to a reporter,
  not an eyewitness account of the examination.
- [FAI's centennial account](https://www.fai.org/news/100-years-fai-granted-bessie-colemans-pilot-licence),
  opening licence statement and paragraphs beginning *To achieve her dream*
  and *After booking*. The issuing body's account supports the licence date,
  June 15, 1921, and the broad training sequence. It does not provide a
  particular post-accident lesson or an observed examination. Its broad
  biography is not independent evidence for Coleman's unrecorded sensations.

Targeted English and French searches for the training, examination and
1921 newspaper coverage located more retellings and references to another
Defender article, *Chicago Girl is Full-Fledged Aviator Now* (October 1,
1921). That original page was not obtained or inspected in this pass. General
descriptions of pilot tests and aircraft controls do not establish what
Coleman did at a particular moment. They were not added to the story.

### Why the available repairs fail

| Proposed repair | Why it cannot clear the hold |
|---|---|
| Write the walk as the next morning after the death. | The source gives a daily habit, not that sequence or occasion. This would invent chronology. |
| Expand shock into trembling hands, sleeplessness, or a decision to face death. | Those particular reactions are not in the inspected testimony. An attested shock is not permission to add a new event or decision. |
| Stage the licence test as a detailed flight. | The testimony gives taking and passing the examination. A general test requirement cannot supply her individual actions, weather, instructor exchanges, or order of manoeuvres. |
| Make her return-home interview the turning point. | It is a real located encounter, but it does not document the event that changed the training situation. Relabeling it would conceal the same gap. |

Needed for this episode: a source locating an individual act of resumed
training after the death, and an account that can render the examination or
another actual turn without invention. A fuller immediate response would
also be needed to deepen the low point. This is a bounded finding about the
material inspected, not a claim that no such record exists.

The later 1923 accident and return-to-work period surfaced as a possible
different research subject. It is not a repair of the currently identified
1920–1921 training episode: it changes age, dates, emotional shape and
matching scope. No later material was transplanted into this draft. The
current draft's ages 28–29 still differ from installed ages 24–29; a draft
age bound does not authorize changing production matching.

## Coltrane

### Material actually reopened

- [August Blume's interview, The Jazz Review, January 1959, printed page 25](https://www.jazzstudiesonline.org/files/jso/resources/pdf/JREVTwo1.pdf).
  The whole rendered page was visually inspected. The central-column
  rehearsal answer describes a repeated practice: home visits, getting the
  pianist up, taking out the horn, working through music, and consulting
  written notes. The top-right continuation supplies a real request to learn
  a previously recorded tune. The next answer describes loneliness when the
  pianist left during performances. These are not a located dismissal scene.
  Visibly corrupted portions were not reconstructed.
- [Coltrane with Don DeMicheal, DownBeat, September 29, 1960](https://downbeat.com/microsites/prestige/trane-interview.html),
  the complete interview through its closing paragraph. The paragraphs
  beginning *In 1955*, *Working with Monk*, and *After leaving Monk* support
  the employment sequence and learning at the piano. They do not supply the
  missing firing occasion. Later technical difficulties belong to their own
  chronology and were not moved backward into 1957.
- [Slought's archive entry for the June 15, 1958 Blume interview](https://slought.org/resources/interview_john_coltrane),
  opening paragraph and *Playlist* entry. This is a useful primary-record
  lead: the archive identifies the audio, its recording setting, and the
  published excerpt. The [official Coltrane interview index](https://www.johncoltrane.com/interviews)
  also lists this recording. **The complete audio was not listened to or
  transcribed in this pass.** The archive description cannot be cited as if
  it established the contents of the uninspected recording.

Targeted searches for Coltrane's immediate reaction to dismissal repeatedly
led to summaries or later quotations. The much-repeated shame quotation
points to Kitty Grime's 1961 interview about his early work with Davis, not
to an established reaction at the 1957 dismissal. The original Grime page was
not obtained here. It supplies no adopted new fact, emotion or scene. The
exact locating lead is *John Coltrane Talks to Jazz News*, December 27, 1961,
page 13; it needs direct inspection before use even for its own period.

### Why the available repairs fail

| Proposed repair | Why it cannot clear the hold |
|---|---|
| Put shame or inadequacy into the firing scene. | Later criticism of earlier recordings does not establish that feeling at dismissal. |
| Present one representative rehearsal as a single remembered visit. | The inspected account is recurrent. Changing tense does not create an attested occasion. |
| Move the performance loneliness into the job-loss passage. | It concerns playing with Monk, after the change of employment. It is not evidence of the firing's immediate emotional impact. |
| Add a home withdrawal scene or make a lesson cause recovery. | Neither action, setting nor causal link is established by the inspected evidence; this would also restore the rejected cure plot. |

Needed for this episode: a contemporaneous or explicitly qualified firsthand
account locating his own immediate experience of dismissal, plus an actual
single response occasion. The full Blume audio is a concrete next primary
source to inspect; no clearance should be inferred before that work. The
current 30–31 age range is unchanged, but the legacy withdrawal-and-cure
matching description remains a separate unresolved content decision.

### Follow-through on the complete Blume recording

The parent requested a concrete access attempt before closing this lead.
The live archive HTML was retrieved successfully. Its play link resolves to
[the original MP3](https://slought.org/media/files/1161-2003-11-15-1-coltrane.mp3);
its separate download link names the same file. A read-only HTTP HEAD request
returned status 200, `audio/mpeg`, and 19,305,908 bytes. The audio is therefore
accessible; this is **not** a missing-file or permission diagnosis.

The page provides no full transcript or caption track; its transcription
reference is to the already inspected one-page excerpt. Opening the MP3 and
the download endpoint with the web reader returned internal errors rather
than audio or transcript content. Discovery of all enabled tools found no
audio-understanding or transcription tool. A local runtime check found no
Whisper implementation, audio decoder, or cached speech model. No transcript
was invented, and no new packages, model downloads, paid service, or account
access were introduced for this bounded investigation.

Searches for a published full transcription led to Chris DeVito's interview
collection. The [publisher/distributor's book page](https://www.ipgbook.com/coltrane-on-coltrane-products-9781556520044.php)
was readable through the web reader and confirms the collection includes
transcribed interviews; its exposed links contain no excerpt of this
interview. Direct HTML retrieval encountered an anti-bot challenge and was
not bypassed. No purchase or access-controlled copy was attempted. Secondary
quotations of the longer Blume conversation were useful locating leads, not
a replacement for checking the primary words in context.

Result: the full recording still requires actual listening or a reliable
transcription checked against the audio. Its uninspected portions are not
declared irrelevant and are not declared evidence that the hold can be
cleared. Coleman's additional October 1 newspaper lead likewise remained a
reference to an unobtained original after targeted title searches.
