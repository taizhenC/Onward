## Newly located primary account

[Jones's Guideposts essay](https://guideposts.org/articles/positive-living/entertainment/movies-and-tv/guideposts-classics-james-earl-jones-on-the-importance-of-mentoring/)
is credited to him and dated November 1993. Locators: paragraphs beginning
`Since I was eight`, `Oh, I could talk`, `When he learned`, `I shook my head`,
and `Then he tricked me`, through the recital.

New particulars: a head-shaking refusal; sustained writing; submission and
waiting for feedback. Shame and difficult greetings remain habitual. The
accusation, protest, walk forward, and recital occupy one encounter. No
unsuccessful practice or delay intervenes.

Its trick, applause, and disappearing-stutter account needs qualification:
other accounts leave the teacher's intent uncertain and preserve continuing
stuttering. We cannot select a more triumphant memory merely to improve plot.

## Cross-checks and chronological limits

I reopened Jones's own answers in the [Academy of Achievement interview,
June 29, 1996](https://achievement.org/achiever/james-earl-jones/), from the
question about the move through the recital and its immediate feeling. The
opening answers identify repeated written schoolwork and accepted quiet.
The answers to `What was the poem he finally got you to read aloud?` and
`What came over you at that moment?` leave the teacher's strategy uncertain
and describe shock rather than uncomplicated happiness. The retrospective
account is evidence with memory limits, not contemporaneous documentation.

The [1993 Fresh Air interview, rebroadcast in 2002](https://freshairarchive.org/segments/actor-james-earl-jones),
Jones section from the family-and-farm questions through `So does the stutter
come back very much for you?`, again places accepting the challenge directly
beside the recital. It supports leaving Sunday school and communicating at
home, but does not locate one withdrawal conversation or a later practice
setback. The archive calls its text a rush transcript; no audio was inspected.

The [2005 TIME first-person essay](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,1071262,00.html),
opening paragraphs, likewise connects the challenge directly with recitation.
It does not supply a missing interval between them.

Three possible rearrangements were rejected:

- Start with the teacher's request and refusal: an actual encounter, but the
  source does not establish the required felt low point within it. Separate
  events for scene, dark and response are not required.
- Make the copying accusation the low point: it is located, but moving a
  long struggle between that accusation and its immediate answer alters the
  reported sequence.
- Keep the childhood opening and insert the essay's poem-writing work:
  that strengthens one compression but leaves the opening, dark, and
  withdrawal passages as recurring circumstances. Effort on the poem is
  not evidence that writing or rehearsing it failed.

An additional challenge from the root editor correctly distinguished an
unsuccessful expectation of feedback from failed writing. That expectation
could support a struggle passage after the refusal, without inventing a
rejected poem. This repairs the proposed sequence, not its missing inward
low point. The teacher's wishes do not establish the boy's feeling in that
encounter; earlier habitual shame cannot silently be moved into it.

## Access limits and useful next sources

[Voices and Silences, 2002 edition, Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=0eNP3HFLqfQC)
exposed catalogue information and the contents: `The Promised Land Site
Unseen` starts at page 33 and `Learning to Speak` at page 59. The attempted
page links did not yield readable pages in this investigation. I have **not**
read those chapters and do not cite a review's quotations as if I had.

[Charlie Rose's September 17, 1993 interview](https://charlierose.com/videos/18970)
was also located. Its public page loads an associated
[transcript endpoint](https://charlierose.com/video/transcripts/18970).
The returned text has visibly incomplete phrases and weak time segmentation.
It repeats the recital account but is unsuitable for installing additional
precise scene facts without checking the recording. No recording was watched.

The [HistoryMakers finding aid](https://www.thehistorymakers.org/sites/default/files/A2016_007_EAD.pdf)
appeared in search as an index to a 2016 oral history, including childhood and
the teacher. The PDF and underlying interview were **not** inspected; this is
an access lead, not corroboration or a fact source for a candidate.

The next useful step is lawful access to the memoir chapters or the complete
oral-history recording, looking for the missing occasions and sequence.
An adult acting episode would be a different age/emotional match requiring a
new episode review; it cannot silently replace the 6–14 childhood stage.
