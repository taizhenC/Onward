## Decision

- **Lamarr: HOLD, R2.** Strong contemporary documents establish a partial patent-claim rejection, urgent forms, her signed affidavit and check, a signature-name defect, its correction, and eventual grant. They do not establish a personal lowest moment, a scene of her receiving or reading a letter, or an emotional change at grant. The 488-word documentary draft preserves the discovery without manufacturing those missing links.
- **Lee: HOLD, historical boundary + R2/R3.** The early teaching gift is supportable, but its exact 1931/1932 dating needs reconciliation. No source read establishes the seeded inherited desk, condolence letter, reply, visits, rejected offers, negotiation count, or crate-opening. Seven documentary passages are not a cleared story. At 402 words, the draft deliberately stops short of padding an unsupported arc.
- **Lewis: substantive source-led draft for independent review, not approval.** The 696-word candidate uses the grief notes themselves. The particular morning recollection, an attempted conversation, doubts about writing, and later return of distress supply actual events and failed effort. The writing anchor must still be judged under the scene rule; no exact room or single continuous notebook session has been established.

A schema pass is not historical or craft clearance. Lamarr and Lee must not be installed merely because they pass mechanical checks.

## Clive Staples Lewis

### Primary account and bounded locators

Read Parts I–IV of [A Grief Observed](https://www.gutenberg.ca/ebooks/lewiscs-agriefobserved/lewiscs-agriefobserved-00-h.html), the Project Gutenberg Canada transcription based on the 1964 Faber edition, first published 1961. This is the author's near-contemporary first-person account, not a later biographer's reconstructed room. Its own claims about mind, belief and relief remain subjective.

- **Scene:** Part I opening “No one ever told me,” next paragraph “At other times,” and “And no one ever told me about the laziness”: bodily symptoms, desired company, dread of an empty house, and difficulty with ordinary effort. Writing about those symptoms is attested; their exact concurrence with the act of writing is not.
- **Dark:** Part I “Meanwhile, where is God?” through “Not that I am”; the paragraph “I tried to put some of these thoughts to C. this afternoon”; and “After the death of a friend, years ago.” These document fear about the character of God, an unsuccessful afternoon conversation, and unanswered appeals for reassurance. The latter two are explicitly prior recollections in the candidate.
- **Response:** Part I “Or are the boys right?” and “I not only live each endless day”: returning to the notebook, doubts about worsening fixation, insufficient distraction from reading, and the author's stated small distance through writing. No publication motive is asserted.
- **Struggle:** Part II opening reread, “I must think more about H.,” “Yes, that sounds very well,” and the meeting “Today I had to meet a man.” He is distressed by self-centered notes and by the gap between remembered and real people. Part I “I cannot talk to the children” gives an actual failed conversational effort; it is not a narrator-imposed diagnosis of the children.
- **Turn:** Part III “Something quite unexpected has happened,” “And suddenly at the very moment,” and “Why has no one told me these things?” A particular early morning, better sleep, lighter feeling, sun and breeze, vivid recall, and the subsequent written examination are documented. Physical recovery is tentative. He stops short of calling the experience a meeting.
- **Became:** Part III “An admirable programme” records the same-night return of bitterness, stomach flutter and tears. Part IV opening describes the fourth and last nearly empty notebook, old arithmetic at its back, a decision not to buy another, and changing grief. “Here, for instance” gives the painful walks; “Still, there are the two enormous gains” refuses to call improvement lasting.

The [C. S. Lewis Foundation timeline](https://www.cslewis.org/resource/chronocsl/) was read for bounded entries 1898, 1950, 1960 and 1963: birth 29 November 1898, wife's death 13 July 1960, literary identification and later death. It is institutional secondary chronology. The candidate does not adopt its wedding chronology.

### Correction before freeze

Root identified two cross-time constructions in the first version. The corrected opening now reports writing about swallowing, not swallowing at that exact writing instant. The dark passage no longer joins Part I belief distress with Part II illness/remission recall as if one occasion. It uses Part I's actual written examination and visibly past afternoon conversation. No room, chair or hour is added. End date 1961-12-31 is explicitly a bibliographical publication envelope, **not** the dated completion of the fourth notebook.

These repairs reduce invented concurrence; they do not prove a single uninterrupted writing session. Independent review should decide whether this bounded writing encounter carries the first roles sufficiently. The actual morning turn is stronger event evidence.

Age 61–62 differs from installed 60–62. Grief, solitude and self-doubt remain aligned, but the prose does not claim that all his earlier theology failed or that writing was undertaken to teach others. The morning is temporary relief, not a cure or theological lesson for the reader. No self-harm method or graphic cancer detail from the wider primary text is reproduced.
