## Mary Shelley

### Accessed evidence

[Marshall, *The Life and Letters of Mary Wollstonecraft Shelley*, volume II (1889)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/37956/pg37956-images.html) was read as public-domain HTML with printed-page markers. The dated letters are contemporary primary testimony reproduced by a later editor. I did not inspect the original manuscripts or authenticate the transcription. Editorial interpretations of Jane Williams and other people were not adopted.

[Volume I](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/37955/pg37955-images.html), printed p. 4, provides birth on 30 August 1797. This editorial chronology establishes the ages; it is not a birth-register inspection.

### Bounded claim map

- Scene, low and response: Shelley to Maria Gisborne, **17 September 1822, pp. 32–34**. She is alone except for her sleeping son. Jane Williams left that morning. The letter describes a newly rented shared house, remaining funds and the cost of returning to England, then turns to grief.
- Distress: **p. 33**, paragraphs beginning “Those about me” and “There are moments”. Her own account supports isolation, perceived misunderstanding and writing through distress. The source's method-specific suicidal language is entirely excluded from the narrative. The draft does not invent a diagnosis, mirror, tears, desk or physical reaction.
- Small response: **p. 33**, the same writing passage through the goodnight paragraph. Her intention to look at the stars stays an intention. The source contains two goodnights; the draft does not claim she actually left the room.
- Important temporal boundary: the requests for a reply and financial help follow an announced break. They appear in the compressed struggle, **not** as if the entire letter were one uninterrupted sitting.
- Real failed effort: Shelley to Clare Clairmont, **20 December 1822, pp. 54–56**. One lawyer's request for an interview was declined; he had to apply in writing and she still lacked a result. The smoking stove and cold room are her own report.
- Conditional child-support offer and refusal: Sir Timothy Shelley to Byron, **6 February 1823, pp. 65–66**, followed by Mary's **undated reply, pp. 67–68**. She refuses separation from her son. No exact reply day or delivery scene is invented.
- Turn: Shelley to Leigh Hunt, **9 September 1823, pp. 95–96**, the paragraph beginning “On the strength of the drama” and the next two paragraphs. Her father accompanies her to Whitton. The advance for immediate expenses is actual; yearly support and an allowance for her remain uncertain. Whitton's wish to stop direct applications is her impression, signaled with “seemed.”
- Became: same letter, **pp. 94, 96–97**, new cheap lodgings, boy in bed, fire, ability to write, relief of financial anxiety, and continuing sadness. No claim of recovery.
- Later difficulty in the fold only: **22 August 1824 letter to Hunt, pp. 120–122**, publication restrictions tied to support. This does not extend the narrative endpoint.
- Reveal: her signature/name and her own discussion of *Frankenstein* in the September 1823 letter.

### Matching and editorial limits

The installed 1818–1823 stage includes deaths of children and a longer grief arc. This draft explicitly narrows to widowhood and support negotiations in 1822–1823; it does not quietly retell the earlier child deaths. The endpoint is after her twenty-sixth birthday, so the installed upper age of twenty-five is inaccurate for this narrative. Root was notified. No matching edit is made here, and approval of this draft would not by itself authorize one.

Money is not a cure for bereavement. Refusing the earlier proposal is not presented as the cause of the later advance. She was not without all funds or help. The shorter-than-target individual low/turn passages were not enlarged with invented feeling or office details; the complete story is 705 words.

## Nina Simone

### Accessed primary and owning evidence

- [Estate biography](https://www.ninasimone.com/biography/): birth and early teaching/bar-work paragraphs.
- [Estate 1933–1959 timeline](https://www.ninasimone.com/1933-1959/): 1950–1954 entries. Retrospective owning-estate chronology, not original school records.
- [Estate press archive](https://www.ninasimone.com/1960-1969/): **Rogue, March 1960**, the direct hiring recollection; **Sepia, October 1960, “Little Girl Blue”**, studio work and summer job loss; **Newsday, Bob Micklin, 24 January 1969**, interview conducted 15 January. Transcriptions read; original page scans were not verified.
- [Peter Dobrin's original archival reporting](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/entertainment/arts/20150816_Curtis_and_the_case_of_Nina_Simone.html): audition date, school documents and interviews. Direct source testimony is distinguished from the writer's preferred explanation. I did not see the underlying audition file.
- [Memoir excerpt reproduced by Adam Shatz](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2016/03/10/fierce-courage-nina-simone/): the first-person passage in the paragraph beginning “Curtis’s rejection” supports remembered humiliation, anger and self-doubt. **The complete memoir was not accessed.**
- [Lucy O'Brien's 1992 interview](https://boscarol.com/ninasimone/pages/nina/articles/1992_Guardian.html): first-person complaint about touring and payment, used only as later fold context. The article's incorrect birth year is not adopted.
- [Juilliard's own account](https://www.juilliard.edu/news/136131/new-jazz-vocalist-program-ramps): identifies her 1950 summer study. A graduate claim in an early press profile is not used.

### What is and is not established

The factual spine is audition rejection, continued private study and paid music work, summer studio closure, and bar employment. The low is explicitly remembered; prejudice is her attributed belief, not a proved institutional motive. Institutional denials likewise do not prove absence of bias.

The two bar accounts cannot be silently combined. The 1960 interview describes agreeing to a singing requirement to obtain the job. The 1969 recollection instead puts the demand after the first night and its surrounding article gives a different year. The held draft consistently uses the earlier hiring version, with `probable` confidence and memory framing, while retaining the conflict in the fold.

The draft does **not** assert a dramatic first-night ultimatum, a first-ever discovery of singing, a Juilliard degree, study as an admitted Curtis pupil, or an eventual hit as proof that rejection was beneficial.

### Remaining HOLD

The rejection's emotional aftermath and the start of private lessons are not securely located short scenes. Inventing a letter, room, walk, first lesson or body gesture would not fix that. Original scans and the full memoir would help test the hiring sequence, but their absence is not concealed. The 371-word seven-part draft is not a recipe-complete story. Episode/matching bounds remain inside the installed 1950–1954 and 17–21 range.

## Harland Sanders

### Accessed evidence and access limitations

- [KFC's owning-company history](https://global.kfc.com/our-history): birth and **1952**, **1956** timeline entries. This establishes that franchising preceded the restaurant sale.
- [1990 National Register nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/7ce8ab5e-d648-4ca8-b640-bdc4b77c11a6): **section 8, printed p. 6 / PDF p. 15**. Text extraction read; page screenshot requests did not produce a viewable image in this tool session. Its historical narrative cites Sanders's 1974 memoir. The PDF skill prompted a visual check attempt; it did not turn the extraction into verified original-memoir access.
- [Sanders, 1966 manuscript published by KFC in 2012](https://kfc.ro/documente/Cartea_Colonelului.pdf): search-indexed text for **pp. 65, 67–68** was accessible. Direct official PDF access failed with HTTP 403. This is an explicit primary-access HOLD, not a claim to have read the full book. A public S3 copy returned only twenty pages and apparent unrelated commercial markings; it was not treated as a verified complete replacement.
- [William Whitworth's 1970 interview/profile](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1970/02/14/colonel-sanders-profile-kentucky-fried-chicken): early road-demonstration paragraphs. This is retrospective, not a dated first-call record.

The indexed memoir provides a disappointing brochure campaign and a later advertisement/inquiry sequence. It does not securely place those episodes after the 1956 auction. The Canadian inquiry therefore cannot yet serve as an approved chronological turning point. The candidate's provisional 65–66 bounds are not certified for these undated passages.

### Excluded versions

No invented suicide scene, exact rejection count, overnight highway catastrophe or conversion of an earlier property offer into a debt. No claim of literally no assets or help. No claim that franchise work began only after the sale. The stronger 1952 Harman cooking scene is **not** substituted for the installed 1956 stage.

### Remaining HOLD

Direct primary pages, calendar placement and actual located low/response/turn moments remain missing. The 312-word candidate is a structured record of the supported spine and its gaps, not a completed production story. Owner review should see the gap, not a padded triumph.
