### 1. Scene

She arrived at the school when she was fourteen, unable to read or write her own name. The other girls had already learned things she had never been taught. She was a new pupil.

### 2. Dark moment

Other pupils laughed when she could not spell simple words. The school's account describes how keenly she felt these humiliations. Her lack of schooling had become something other people could mock. She felt ashamed.

### 3. Response

She began learning the skills that the other pupils already had. In her later account, she remembered learning quickly. She learned to read.

### 4. Struggle

School did not become easy all at once. She broke rules, and her temper brought her close to being sent away. Staff who knew her intervened. A house mother became especially close to her. As her studies advanced, a weekly class in plays became a source of pleasure. She later remembered the force of those words long after the lessons ended. Reading had become more than a test.

### 5. Turning point

Six years after arriving, she delivered the closing address for her graduating class. She thanked the teachers and staff who had helped them. She spoke about friendships made at the school and learning that would continue beyond it. She was twenty now.

### 6. Became

In that address, she described school as the beginning of work that would continue through life. The gratitude was hers, spoken to the people who had taught her. Her next place in the world was less certain. The school records her fear that she might have to return to the institution she had left. Graduation had not supplied a job.

### 7. Bridge

Her name was Anne Sullivan.

She became the teacher whose work with Helen Keller helped transform education for people who are deafblind.

### 3. Response

A small group nevertheless began to meet and share the work. After about three months, he went home and looked again for paid work. He also continued seeking people who wanted help with their drinking. The visits went on.
